Greyscale (lightness) check?

Hints for geoscience figures

  • Rainbow / jet on attribute maps. Red, green and yellow collapse for red-green deficiencies and lightness goes up and down, so the same value can look like two things and two values like one. Prefer a map with steadily rising lightness (viridis, cividis or a grey ramp) and test it here.
  • Red / green well-log curves. Two thin lines in red and green merge for most colour-blind readers. Vary line style (solid, dashed), width or markers, and use colours that differ in lightness, such as dark blue and orange.
  • Fault traces and picks. A thin red or green line over a rainbow map dissolves. Use black or white outlines (a halo), thicker lines, or a colour that sits far from the background in lightness.
  • Seismic red-white-blue. Red-blue is usually safe for red-green deficiencies, but check that the two polarities differ in lightness, and check the greyscale result because many prints are black and white.
  • Categorical legends (lithology, facies). More than 5 to 6 colours get confused quickly. Add patterns, labels or symbols; a palette designed for colour-blind readers such as Okabe-Ito helps, but does not remove the need for a second cue.
  • Do not rely on one test. This is a model of an average observer for a given type and severity; real people vary, and screens and print differ. Treat it as a screening check, not a certification.
